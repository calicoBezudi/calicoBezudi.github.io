Garden of Idan

An Isometric puzzle game where the player explores a dark, blossoming mansion in search of an enigmatic figure from her dreams. Rotate the camera to solve puzzles and follow in the footsteps of your mother.

Designing many of the core systems for Garden of Idan proved to be a technical challenge that I was not originally expecting. We developed the game in Unreal Engine 5.6, and I was able to take advantage of a few key systems introduced during this version of the engine in order to overcome some of the challenges I faced. Overall, I produced a strong systems standards and practices workflow, taught my team how to work within Perforce, and designed complex key systems such as the isometric camera system, a modular puzzle management system, a UI manager with a priority system, a save system, and many other things such as light reflecting puzzle systems. I also developed a custom EQS query for enemy AI and combat, which unfortunately did not make it into the final project.
This was my original concept sketch to capture how the camera rotation would affect the environment. It is very basic, but outlining these things is important to portray and get everyone on the same page on how things will be developed. As part of the Three C's (Camera, Controls, Character), outlining these mechanics is very important in understanding how they will interact with the game. Aspects such as whether the camera will freely rotate, what will need to be occluded for the camera, the angle of the camera, and even where the rotation should be centered are all questions that greatly affect how the system will need to be designed at the end of the day. They also have a not insiginificant impact on gameplay. Centering the camera on the player proved to get players lost far too often in their environment, so a solution that was centered on the room was the best way to keep player's spatial awareness.

This video is a demonstration of the custom EQS query I created. It keeps track of how frequent a point has been visited, and uses that in the weighting as well as distance to calculate a reasonable path for an enemy to follow. This system supports multiple enemies and is easy for designers to create enemy rooms with. Enemies will path between said points intelligently, and each point can be dragged and dropped into a room with a navmesh and an enemy and begin working immediately.
